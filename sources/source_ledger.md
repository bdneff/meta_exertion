# Source Ledger

Every row in the master dataset links to an entry here. Each entry records the
exact publication/report, the page/table, the access status, and the verbatim
figures extracted. "VERIFIED" = the number was read directly from the primary
source (or its PMC full text / official PDF) during this project. "SECONDARY" =
taken from an abstract or reputable secondary source, primary not fully
accessed. "NOT VERIFIED" = located but the number could not be confirmed.

## S01 — Yeargin et al. 2019, NCAA Exertional Heat Illness (ANCHOR, NCAA)
- **Citation:** Yeargin SW, Dompier TP, Casa DJ, Hirschhorn RM, Kerr ZY.
  Epidemiology of Exertional Heat Illnesses in NCAA Athletes During the
  2009–2010 Through 2014–2015 Academic Years. *J Athl Train.* 2019;54(1):55–63.
  PMID 30668925. PMC6410980. DOI 10.4085/1062-6050-volumnumber-issuenumber.
- **Access:** VERIFIED via PMC full text.
- **Sample:** 166 NCAA institutions, 2048 team-seasons, 25 sports. NCAA-ISP
  convenience sample (not universal; ~ up to ~20% of programs).
- **Denominator:** 4,908,478 athlete-exposures (AE) total.
- **Key figures (VERIFIED):**
  - Total EHI: **232 events**, overall rate **0.47/10,000 AE (95% CI 0.41–0.53)**.
  - Football: **174 events**, **1.55/10,000 AE (1.32–1.78)**; 75% of all EHI.
  - Preseason practice: **1.16/10,000 AE**; all other periods **0.23/10,000 AE**
    (IRR 4.96, 3.79–6.50). Preseason = 64.7% (n=150) of EHI. Postseason
    0.03/10,000 AE.
  - **Emergency transport required: 19 of 232 (8%).**  ← serious-endpoint anchor
  - Diagnoses: heat cramps 38.8%, dehydration 28.9%, heat exhaustion 27.2%,
    1 exertional heat stroke, 3 hyponatremia.
  - Severity: 35% no time loss (NTL), 61% 1–6 days.
  - Sport counts/rates per 10,000 AE (95% CI):
    - M football 174, 1.55 (1.32–1.78)
    - M basketball 12, 0.41 (0.18–0.65)
    - W soccer 8, 0.30 (0.09–0.50)
    - M soccer 6, 0.31 (0.06–0.56)
    - W outdoor track 6, 0.59 (0.12–1.07)
    - M baseball 5, 0.22 (0.03–0.41)
    - M wrestling 3, 0.29 (0.00–0.61)
    - M cross-country 3, 0.48 (0.00–1.02)
    - W cross-country 2, 0.35 (0.00–0.84)
    - M ice hockey 2, 0.05 (0.00–0.12)
    - W tennis 2, 0.43 (0.00–1.03)
    - W basketball 2, 0.08 (0.00–0.19)
    - W lacrosse 1, 0.07 (0.00–0.20)
    - W field hockey 1, 0.20; W indoor track 1, 0.07; W swim/dive 1, 0.06;
      W volleyball 1, 0.05; W softball 1, 0.05; W ice hockey 1, 0.07.
    - Zero EHI: M indoor track, M lacrosse, M outdoor track, M swim/dive,
      M tennis, W gymnastics.
- **Reconstructed AE by sport** (AE = count/rate×10,000): football ≈ 1,122,581.
- **Notes:** Case definition = EHI evaluated by athletic trainer/physician,
  reportable regardless of time loss. Rate denominator counts practices+games
  as AE. This is a *diagnosis-level* (secondary-endpoint) source; the 8%
  transport subset feeds the primary (serious) endpoint.

## S02 — MSMR Exertional Rhabdomyolysis 2020–2024 (ANCHOR, Military)
- **Citation:** Exertional Rhabdomyolysis Among Active Component Members of the
  U.S. Armed Forces, 2020–2024. *MSMR* 2025. PMID 40591365. PMC12266732.
- **Access:** VERIFIED via PMC full text.
- **Case definition:** ICD-9/10 rhabdomyolysis (728.88/M62.82) or myoglobinuria
  (791.3/R82.1) in any position PLUS an associated exertion/heat code. Includes
  **both inpatient and outpatient** encounters.
- **Key figures (VERIFIED):**
  - Annual: 2020 n=518 (38.0/100k p-yr); 2021 n=526 (38.4); 2022 n=520 (38.2);
    2023 n=512 (39.7); 2024 n=464 (35.9/100k p-yr).
  - **By rank (2024): Recruit trainees n=95, 410.4/100,000 p-yr** (>13× others);
    enlisted 30.2; officer 23.9.  ← recruit anchor
  - By service (2024): USMC 84.7 (n=143), Army 53.8 (n=237), Navy 12.7 (n=41),
    USAF 11.8 (n=38), USCG 12.6 (n=5).
  - By age (2024): <20 yr 83.9/100k (n=125); 20–24 yr 40.9 (n=131).
  - By sex (2024): male 40.3/100k (n=429), female 15.2 (n=35).
  - **Hospitalized fraction: 2024 = 39.7% (184/464); 5-yr avg 38.8%.**
    ← inpatient split for the serious endpoint
  - Installations 2020–24 (recruit bases): MCRD Parris Island n=211 (8.3%),
    Fort Benning n=191 (7.5%), JBSA-Lackland n=72 (2.8%), Fort Leonard Wood
    n=60 (2.4%); Fort Bragg n=278 (11.0%, mixed active/airborne).
- **Notes:** Person-years denominator = whole calendar-year service time, NOT
  training minutes — must be converted for participant-minute analysis.
  "Recruit trainee" person-time ≈ time in initial entry only.

## S03 — Scoville et al. 2004, Nontraumatic deaths in basic training (Military death anchor)
- **Citation:** Scoville SL, Gardner JW, Magill AJ, Potter RN, Kark JA.
  Nontraumatic deaths during U.S. Armed Forces basic training, 1977–2001.
  *Am J Prev Med.* 2004;26(3):205–212. PMID 15026099.
- **Access:** SECONDARY (abstract + DTIC ADA421540 summary; full text paywalled,
  DTIC PDF returned 403). Recruit-year denominator PENDING from recruit agent.
- **Key figures (from abstract/summary, VERIFIED at abstract level):**
  - 276 recruit deaths 1977–2001; 72% nontraumatic (n≈199).
  - **139 of 199 nontraumatic deaths (70%) exercise-related.**
  - Exercise deaths: 59 (42%) cardiac; heat stress primary/contributory in ≥46 (33%).
  - Rates computed per 100,000 recruit-years using DMDC accession data.
  - Nontraumatic RR 2.5 for age 25+ vs <25; 2.6× higher for African-American recruits.
- **Notes:** Denominator (recruit-years) must be reconstructed from accession
  throughput × course length; flagged for the recruit-throughput source.

## S04 — MSMR Heat Exhaustion & Heat Stroke 2020–2024 (Military heat anchor)
- **Citation:** Heat Exhaustion and Heat Stroke Among Active Component Members of
  the U.S. Armed Forces, 2020–2024. *MSMR* 2025. PMC12266733.
- **Access:** VERIFIED via PMC full text.
- **Case def:** Heat stroke ICD-9 992.0 / ICD-10 T67.0*; heat exhaustion
  ICD-9 992.3–992.5 / ICD-10 T67.3–T67.5; inpatient OR outpatient OR reportable
  medical event; counted once/person/calendar year.
- **Key figures (VERIFIED, per 100,000 person-years):**
  - Heat stroke: 2020 n=477 (35.0); 2021 n=404 (31.2); 2022 n=425 (32.8);
    2023 n=408 (31.2); 2024 n=471 (36.4).
  - Heat exhaustion: 2020 n=1646 (120.8); 2021 n=1865 (139.5); 2022 n=2037
    (150.7); 2023 n=2261 (173.3); 2024 n=2380 (183.9).
  - **Recruit trainees 2024: heat stroke 116.6/100k (n=27); heat exhaustion
    2388.9/100k (n=553).**  ← recruit heat anchors
  - Service 2024 heat stroke: USMC 84.7, Army 55.8, Navy 13.0, USAF 12.5.
  - Age <20 (2024): heat stroke 104.9/100k (n=86); heat exhaustion 761.2 (n=624).
  - **Heat stroke hospitalized 2024: 167/471 = 35.5% inpatient (64.5% outpatient);
    2023 = 39.7%.**  ← inpatient split
  - Top installation: Fort Benning n=1839 (14.8% of 2020–24 heat illness);
    Parris Island n=642 (5.2%).
- **Notes:** Recruit heat stroke and heat exhaustion are the richest military
  serious/heat endpoints. Heat stroke ≈ serious by definition. Person-years =
  calendar service time; convert to training minutes for per-minute analysis.

## S04b — Nelson et al. 2016 NEJM, Sickle trait/rhabdo/mortality, Army (supporting)
- **Citation:** Nelson DA, Deuster PA, Carter R, Hill OT, Wolcott VL, Kurina LM.
  Sickle Cell Trait, Rhabdomyolysis, and Mortality among U.S. Army Soldiers.
  *N Engl J Med.* 2016;375(5):435–442. PMID 27518662.
- **Access:** SECONDARY (abstract verified; full text paywalled).
- **Key figures:** Cohort 47,944 black active-duty soldiers. Sickle trait HR for
  exertional rhabdo 1.54 (95% CI 1.12–2.12); no excess mortality (HR 0.99).
- **Notes:** Confirms rhabdo is an active-training phenomenon; supports the
  exertional-sickling stratum. Not a recruit-only denominator.
